The Chimera Creed

A creed is only worth stating if it can be failed. These pillars are written to be tested against a week, a decision, a bad morning — not admired on a wall.

PILLAR I

Sovereignty of Christ

All authority, personal ambition, and earthly systems must ultimately submit to the King of Kings as revealed in Holy Scripture. There is no true sovereignty, no lasting order, and no final meaning apart from Him. This is the absolute anchor; without it, all other principles eventually drift into moral relativism.

PILLAR II

Radical Accountability

A man must own his past, his present, and his future without qualification. The modern reliance on excuses, systemic blame, and victim narratives are the chains of the weak. True agency begins with the terrifying acceptance that a man is solely accountable before God and history for what he builds and what he allows to be destroyed.

PILLAR III

Dominion of Self

Before a man can lead others, protect a family, or build anything of lasting value, he must conquer himself. The disciplined body, the ordered mind, and the tempered will are the bedrock of all external authority. A man who cannot govern his own appetites will inevitably be governed by those who know how to exploit them.

PILLAR IV

Discerning Loyalty

Strength does not exist for self-aggrandizement; its purpose is to shield what is good, true, and ordered. Loyalty, effort, and protection must be fiercely given, but only to the worthy: family bound by covenant, brothers tested by shared adversity, and institutions that still strive for the good. Loyalty without discernment is self-destruction.

PILLAR V

Unclouded Realism

A man must condition himself to see men, women, power structures, and human nature exactly as they are — not as idealism desires them to be. Acknowledging the tragic fallenness of man is not cynicism; it is the prerequisite for effective action and genuine love. Illusions are fatal. Victory can only be built upon the harsh ground of reality.

PILLAR VI

Principled Shrewdness

In a deeply flawed world, the defense of the innocent often requires navigating difficult moral terrain. A man must possess the pragmatism to do what is necessary to protect order, acting as "wise as serpents and innocent as doves." This means engaging with the realities of power without letting the darkness corrupt the soul. Pragmatism in the service of high principles is not a compromise; it is wisdom.

PILLAR VII

Vocation of Renewal

Personal transformation is not an endpoint, nor is it a vanity project. The ultimate purpose of a rebuilt man is the rebuilding of his civilization. By rejecting both the globalist dissolution of identity and the descent into tribal individualism, a man takes up the ultimate vocation: the restoration of ordered liberty under God, ensuring that his nation and his people have a foundation upon which to stand.
